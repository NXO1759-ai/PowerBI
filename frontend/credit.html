The credit view is in build

Equity analysis asks what a business is worth; credit analysis asks whether it can pay. Same filings, different question — and the data spine this terminal already runs (fundamentals refreshed weekly from SEC EDGAR, statements parsed to the line item, segment and risk detail read from each filing) is the spine a credit report needs. What follows is what this page will compute when it opens.

Leverage & coverage

Gross and net leverage through EBITDA and through free cash flow, interest cover on both, and the trend of each across the cycle — the four numbers every credit committee reads first.

Maturity wall

When the debt actually comes due, from the filings' own maturity schedules — refinancing risk is a calendar before it is a ratio.

Liquidity runway

Cash, undrawn capacity where disclosed, and the burn or build against it — how long the company operates if markets shut.

Covenant headroom

Where reported figures sit against the covenant levels filers disclose, and how much deterioration the headroom absorbs.

Priority & recovery

What ranks ahead of what — secured, unsecured, leases, pensions — and what the asset side suggests each layer recovers.

The credit cycle

The same macro feed the strategy page reads — policy rate, curve, claims — pointed at the question credit cares about: what happens to this borrower when refinancing costs what the curve says next.

Everything above will be computed from filings and stated sources the way the rest of the terminal works: every figure traceable, every gap named rather than filled, and an analyst's note arguing what the numbers mean together. Until it opens, the Overview, Statements and Strategy pages already carry the balance-sheet, cash-flow and coverage figures a credit read starts from.
